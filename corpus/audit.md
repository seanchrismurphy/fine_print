## NRMA

**Document version / retrieval date:** Accessed: 2026-04-27

**Overall structure:** Inclusions and exclusions under 'Listed events' (p13)
definitions on P9 under 'definitions' General 'what we cover - what we don't
cover' structure, lists of events and situations, optional benefits after main
benefits

**Key definitions (verbatim + page number):**
- Actions of the see "Actions of the Sea means: – sea waves; and – normal
  movement or changes in the sea levels (including high tides and king
  tides),unless caused by Storm Surge.Actions of the Sea does not include Storm
  Surge." (p9)
- Flood: "Flood means the covering of normally dry land by water that has
  escaped or been released from the normal confines of any of the following: – a
  lake (whether or not it has been altered or modified); – a river (whether or
  not it has been altered or modified); – a creek (whether or not it has been
  altered or modified); – another natural watercourse (whether or not it has
  been altered or modified); – a reservoir; – a canal; or  – a dam" (p. 11)
  exclusions p14
- Storm / storm damage: "Storm means: – rain, thunderstorm, hail or snow; or –
  violent wind, cyclone or tornado" (p. 12)
- Storm surge Storm Surge means an increase in sea level that occurs because of
  an intense Storm or cyclone and associated waves. (p. 12) exclusions p15
- Rainwater / surface water: "Rainwater Run-off means water that flows over the
  ground, or backs-up, as a result of a Storm" (p. 12)
- Escape of water or liquid "We cover loss or damage caused by: – water or
  liquid leaking or escaping from gutters, drainpipes or pipes at the Site; –
  water or liquid leaking or escaping from water mains, water supply pipes,
  baths, spa baths, sinks, toilets, bidets, sanitary fixtures, appliances,
  aquariums, water tanks or swimming pools; – liquid leaking from a fixed
  heating system or sealed portable heater; and – landslide or subsidence that
  happens within 72 hours of, and as a direct result of, liquid leaking as
  covered in this event." (p 14) excluslsions p14

**Exclusions relevant to water damage:** For e.g. Flood "We will not cover: –
loss or damage to: – retaining walls; – gates, fences or free-standing walls if
the loss or damage was caused because they were not structurally sound or
well-maintained; – pontoons, jetties or bridges; – gravel driveways; – swimming
pool covers or spa covers that are more than 5 years old; or – pool and spa
solar covers or liners;  – erosion, deterioration, collapse, shrinkage or any
other earth movement; – Actions of the Sea; or – loss or damage caused by escape
of water or liquid, Storm, Storm Surge or tsunami" (p 14). 

Note: Each section excludes the others, so flood says they won't pay for storm
surge, storm surge says they won't pay for flood - I presume just to avoid
double dipping. 

**Structural notes for chunking:** Each section (e.g. Flood) says they won't
cover damage arising from other sections (e.g. Storm) even though they do cover
that (it says so under the storm section). This is going to be complex to solve.
We probably need a prompt like: "Insurance policies often exclude a peril from
one section because it is covered under a different section. This is not a
contradiction. When retrieved chunks appear to conflict, identify which section
each chunk comes from, determine which section governs the type of event
described in the query, and use that section to determine the coverage outcome.
State explicitly which section your answer is drawn from and why." This means we need
section metadata visible to the model - so it can know it's reading the flood section, not 
the storm section.

## Allianz

**Document version / retrieval date:** Accessed: 2026-04-27

**Overall structure:** Definitions start p79.
Starts with what is/isn't covered in general (e.g. buildings), then the inclusions/exclusions
for covered events like flood. What they pay in what situations (in tables). Optional benefits, 
exclusions, making a claim, process of filing, definitions. 

**Key definitions (verbatim + page number):**
- Actions of the sea: "This includes: • storm surge, • a rise in the level of
the ocean or sea caused by a high tide, a king tide or any other movement of the
sea.Actions of the sea does not include tsunami" (p79)
- Flood: "The covering of normally dry land by water that has escaped or been
released from the normal confines of any of the following: • a lake (whether or
not it has been altered or modified), • a river (whether or not it has been
altered or modified), • a creek (whether or not it has been altered or
modified), • another natural watercourse (whether or not it has been altered or
• modified), • a reservoir, • a canal, or • a dam" (p. X) exclusions p25
- rainwater: "Rain falling naturally from the sky onto your buildings and/or
  ground." exclusions p24
- Storm / storm damage: "Violent wind (including cyclones and tornadoes),
  thunderstorms or hail which may be accompanied by rainwater or snow." (p. 83)
  exclusions p24
- Storm surge: "A rise above the normal water level along a shore resulting from
  strong onshore winds and/or reduced atmospheric pressure." (p 83)
- Sudden escape of liquid  "Loss or damage resulting directly from bursting,
leaking, discharging or overflowing of any: • domestic appliances – for example
a dishwasher or washing machine, • fixed rainwater or hot water tanks, •
radiators and oil heaters, • fixed pipes, gutters or drainpipes, sinks,  baths,
basins or water carrying apparatus, • waterbeds, or • aquariums." (p 27)
exclusions p27
- tsunami: "A wave or waves caused by a sudden movement of the ocean due to
earthquakes, volcanic eruptions or other meteorological disturbances. It does
not include anything we define as actions of the sea." p84

**Exclusions relevant to water damage:** p 24/25/27

**Structural notes for chunking:** Simpler structure - the inclusions/exclusions
are self-contained, then you need the references/defintions section to fully
understand them. Apart from a few tables, fairly consistent paragraph style
throughout, should be easy to chunk in theory - esp if we break on title-case.
